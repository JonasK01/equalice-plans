## Why plan 01 was rejected

The client's verdict was that the plan did not hit the science. It is a fair verdict. The
first version was built on "SUPPORT FOR HEALTHY AGING", "10+ RESEARCHED BOTANICALS", "MADE IN
USA. LAB-TESTED." and "LONGEVITY, BACKED BY SCIENCE." Every one of those lines could be
pasted onto any competitor's bottle without changing a word, while the assets that genuinely
belong to this product were left on the table:

1. **A named scientist as co-founder.** Documented in the listing: *Dr. Bryant Villeponteau,
   Ph.D.* Almost no supplement in this category can put a real longevity researcher's name on
   the pack. It appeared nowhere in the plan.
2. **A peer-reviewed lifespan study** in PLoS One. Extraordinarily rare here. Not used.
3. **A contrarian mechanism** — competitors sell "more stem cells", this brand argues that
   using them up faster is the wrong goal and works on their effectiveness and their
   micro-environment instead. That is a real argument a shopper can weigh. Not used.
4. **"Backed by science"** is the emptiest phrase in the category. Saying it is the opposite
   of showing it, and to a science-led client it reads as an admission that we did not read
   the material.

The rule this establishes for every Life Code plan: **name the science, do not gesture at
it.** A journal name, a researcher's name, a mechanism, a number. Never the word "science" as
a substitute for one.

## The founder credential

*Co-founded by Dr. Bryant Villeponteau, Ph.D.* — documented in the SEO listing.

This is the strongest untapped asset on the listing and should carry a tile of its own.

**Still to confirm with the client before it can be used in copy:** his research background
and institution. A specific, checkable credential ("led the telomerase research team at …",
"author of …") is worth many times more than "Ph.D." on its own, but it must come from the
client in writing. Do not source it from memory and do not infer it.

## Open questions for the client

1. Full SC100+® blend with the milligram amount — the 465 mg figure belongs to the **base**
   formula and must not be printed on the Plus.
2. Dr. Villeponteau's verifiable credential, in the client's own words.
3. Does the PLoS One fruit-fly study cover the Plus formula, or the base only? If the base
   only, the study may still be used, but only as *the SC100® formula this product builds on*.
   Attaching a study to a formula it did not test is exactly the sort of thing that loses a
   science-led client's trust for good.
4. Dosing for the Plus. The base is 2/day; "faster acting" implies this may differ.

## Compliance

All rules in `../02_stem_cell_100/SOURCE_NOTES.md` apply without exception, in particular:
no cancer or organ claims of any kind, no human lifespan claim from the fruit-fly data, and
the "already in the normal range" qualifier wherever glucose, blood pressure or cholesterol
is mentioned.

Telomere and autophagy wording must stay structure/function: "supports telomere health" is
acceptable, "lengthens your telomeres" is a disease-adjacent outcome claim and is not.
